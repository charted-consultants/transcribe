10 Years of Learning & Teaching English — in 25 Minutes

Part 1 — Why You're Stuck 5 min

Open with the question
Ask directly to camera: "You've been studying English for years. Why aren't you fluent yet?" — don't make it rhetorical, answer it. Most people assume not enough vocab, don't study enough, or not talented. None of those are true.
The real reason: the method is wrong
The method was given to them by a system never designed to produce fluent speakers.
Problem 1 — Brain hunts for what it doesn't know
When reading or listening, attention goes straight to unfamiliar words. You stop, try to look them up, lose the thread. You understand nothing — not because you don't know enough, but because you're focused on the gaps. Trained behaviour, not a natural one.
Problem 2 — Trained to fear being wrong
Vietnamese education rewards precision and punishes mistakes. So you only speak when certain. You wait. While you're waiting — everyone else is speaking, getting corrected, improving. You leave exactly where you came in.
Land the point
"You haven't failed English. English has been taught to you wrong. And if you don't fix the method — you can practise for another 10 years and still be in the same place."

Part 2 — The Right Mindset 8 min

80% is enough — the California study
Social researchers spent 6 years studying friendliness in cities globally. Results: cities with less money → friendlier people. Rio #1 despite high crime. Lilongwe #3. Used as a live demo: students guessed 80% of the content using only words they already knew. No lookups. No full comprehension of every sentence. They got the idea.
"With 10–20% of the vocabulary, you can understand 80% of the content. You don't need to know every word. You need to know enough to guess the rest."
The IELTS implication
A 20-minute reading passage — done in 3–5 minutes using this method. Understand the main idea of the whole thing, know which paragraph holds which information, then go back for the specific answer. You save 15 minutes and perform better.
Don't learn vocabulary — learn knowledge
Word lists don't stick without context. When you learn a topic — food, farming, cities, business — the words come with the topic. Every class is built around something real. That's how your brain actually stores language.
"Don't learn vocabulary. Learn knowledge. The words follow the topics."
Good enough is the entry point
Most people wait until they can say it perfectly before they say anything. That's backwards. The first attempt doesn't have to be good — it has to exist. Good enough is how you get to better.
"80% is enough to communicate. 100% is what keeps you silent."
Don't stay silent
Silence gives you nothing. No practice, no feedback, no improvement. The students who improve fastest are the ones who speak constantly. Every word you say, even the wrong ones, is data. Silence is nothing.
"Say something. Anything. Even if it's broken. Because broken gets corrected. Silent gets forgotten."
Perfect practice, not just practice
Repeating the wrong thing a thousand times doesn't make you better — it makes the mistake automatic. This is why years of traditional English study produces very little. Students practise — just the wrong way.
"You haven't been lazy. You've been practising the wrong thing."

Part 3 — The Methods 8 min

Method 1 — The Answer-First Method
Old way: read question → try to figure it out → check answer after. New way: read question → if you don't know within 5 minutes → go straight to the answer → spend the rest of the time understanding why it's the answer.
"The other students were figuring out. I was understanding. Those are not the same thing."
The UK finance story
Topped the hardest course in the programme studying less than everyone. Every exercise: went to the answer first. While others were still working it out, he already understood and could explain it. When the lecturer asked — he put his hand up. Only said the ones he understood. Because he'd spent time understanding them, he could explain them.
Figuring out vs understanding — and applied to English
Figuring out = trying to produce the answer from what you know. Understanding = taking the answer and building the knowledge backwards. Faster, deeper, sticks longer. Applied to English: look at a good example sentence first, understand why it's structured that way, then write your own.
"You're not copying. You're learning the pattern. And patterns are how language actually works."
Method 2 — Messy First Pass
When you write, you're trying to generate and edit at the same time. That's why you freeze. Separate them. Two minutes: write everything, don't think about grammar, don't look at keyboard, don't correct yourself, just produce. Then hand to AI. The first pass doesn't have to be good. It has to exist. You cannot improve something that isn't there yet.
"Don't think. Just write. Just speak. Just go. The mess is the starting point — not the failure."
Method 3 — The Rapid Iteration Loop
Most learners: write → wait → feedback days later → try again. Too slow. Right loop: produce → immediate feedback → understand what to fix → do it again right now. Under 10 minutes. Same topic, better version. In class: 2 minutes writing, ChatGPT feedback prompt, read feedback, write again 2 minutes.
"The people who improve fast aren't the ones who study more. They're the ones who iterate faster."

Part 4 — The Tools 4 min

Record yourself → transcribe → improve
Most people think about improving their speaking but never hear themselves. Record yourself — not the whole class, just you. Upload to transcription tool. Read what you actually said. Paste into ChatGPT: "Show me how to improve this." You'll see things you never noticed — filler sounds, repeated words, awkward phrasing.
"You can't fix what you can't hear. Record it. Read it. Fix it."
Two mini-prompts
Everything you don't understand → ChatGPT immediately. (1) Phrase + "meaning" → explains in context, not a dictionary. (2) Vietnamese sentence + "how to say" → natural English version. For writing feedback: "Give feedback on grammar and vocabulary — don't rewrite for me, just tell me what to fix." The "don't rewrite" part is critical — keeps the learning with you.
Build the reflex
The gap between people who use AI well and people who don't isn't knowledge — it's reflex. Immediately reaching for the tool vs sitting and wondering.
"The reflex that makes you good: anything unclear → ChatGPT immediately. Build that reflex."
Treat it like an employee
You are the manager. ChatGPT is your assistant sitting next to you. If they're doing nothing — that's on you. Not using it fully means your English doesn't grow as fast as it could.
"ChatGPT is your employee. If they're sitting there doing nothing — that's on you."
