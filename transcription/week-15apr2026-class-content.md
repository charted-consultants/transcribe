## "10 Years of Learning and Teaching English — in 25 Minutes"

### Why this works as a video

Most English content on the internet is either:
- Too basic (phrase lists, filler words, pronunciation drills)
- Too academic (grammar rules, textbook exercises)
- Too slow (years of courses to get to the point)

This is none of those. It's one person who learned English the wrong way, figured out what actually works, and has now taught it to hundreds of people — and it fits in 25 minutes.

The promise is specific and believable. Not "learn English fast" — that's vague and oversold. "10 years in 25 minutes" tells the viewer exactly what they're getting: the distilled version of a decade of real experience, skipping everything that doesn't matter.

---

### What the 25 minutes covers

---

**Part 1 — Why You're Stuck (5 min)**

Open with a question: *"You've been studying English for years. Why aren't you fluent yet?"*

Not a rhetorical question — answer it directly. Most people assume they're stuck because they don't have enough vocabulary, or they don't study enough, or they're just not talented at languages. None of those are true.

The real reason: **the method is wrong**. And the method was given to them by a system that was never designed to produce fluent speakers.

Walk through the two problems:

**Problem 1 — The brain hunts for what it doesn't know.** When you read or listen, your attention goes straight to unfamiliar words. You stop. You try to look them up. You lose the thread. You understand nothing — not because you don't know enough, but because you're focused on the gaps instead of what's already there. This is a trained behaviour, not a natural one.

**Problem 2 — Trained to fear being wrong.** Vietnamese education rewards precision and punishes mistakes. So you only speak when you're certain. You don't guess. You don't try. You wait. And while you're waiting — everyone else is speaking, getting corrected, improving. You leave the class exactly where you came in.

Land the point: *"You haven't failed English. English has been taught to you wrong. And if you don't fix the method — you can practise for another 10 years and still be in the same place."*

---

**Part 2 — The Right Mindset (8 min)**

This is the reframe section. Before you give anyone a method, they have to believe the method is worth trying. That means dismantling what they currently believe.

**80% is enough.**
Introduce the California study as a live demo of this: complex text, unfamiliar topic, students guessed 80% of the content using only words they already knew. They didn't look anything up. They didn't understand every sentence. They understood the idea.

*"With 10–20% of the vocabulary, you can understand 80% of the content. You don't need to know every word. You need to know enough to guess the rest."*

The IELTS implication: a 20-minute reading passage, done in 3–5 minutes using this method. Understand the main idea of the whole thing, know which paragraph holds which information, then go back for the specific answer. You save 15 minutes. You perform better.

**Don't learn vocabulary — learn knowledge.**
Word lists don't work because words without context don't stick and can't be used. When you learn a topic — food, farming, cities, business — the words come with the topic. You don't chase words. Words follow knowledge.

*"Every class here is built around something real. Because that's how your brain actually stores language."*

**Good enough is the entry point.**
Most people wait until they can say it perfectly before they say it at all. That's backwards. The first attempt doesn't have to be good — it has to exist. Good enough is how you get to better.

*"80% is enough to communicate. 100% is what keeps you silent."*

**Don't stay silent.**
Silence gives you nothing. No practice, no feedback, no improvement. The students who improve fastest aren't the ones who speak correctly — they're the ones who speak constantly. Every word you say, even the wrong ones, is data. Silence is nothing.

*"Say something. Anything. Even if it's broken. Because broken gets corrected. Silent gets forgotten."*

**Perfect practice, not just practice.**
Repeating the wrong thing a thousand times doesn't make you better — it makes the mistake automatic. This is why years of traditional English study often produces very little: students practise, but they practise the wrong way. The hours only count if the method is right.

*"You haven't been lazy. You've been practising the wrong thing."*

---

**Part 3 — The Methods (8 min)**

Now they're ready for the how. Three methods, each one concrete and immediately usable.

**Method 1 — The Answer-First Method.**
Most people: read the question → try to figure it out → check the answer after. This is slow, inefficient, and demoralising when you can't get it.

The better way: read the question → if you don't know within 5 minutes, go straight to the answer → spend the rest of your time understanding *why* it's the answer.

Personal story: topped a UK accounting and finance course — one of the hardest in the programme — by studying less than everyone else. How? Every exercise, he went to the answer first. While other students were still trying to work it out, he already understood the answer and could explain it. When the lecturer asked the class — he put his hand up.

*"The other students were figuring out. I was understanding. Those are not the same thing."*

Figuring out = trying to produce the answer from what you already know. Understanding = taking the answer and building the knowledge backwards. Understanding is faster, deeper, and sticks longer.

Applied to English: when you don't know how to write a sentence, look at a good example first. Understand why it's structured that way. Then write your own. That's not copying — that's how language is actually learned.

**Method 2 — Messy First Pass.**
When you write, you're trying to do two things at once: generate ideas and edit them. That's why you freeze, write slowly, or produce very little.

Separate them. Two minutes — write everything, don't think about grammar, don't look at the keyboard, don't correct yourself, just produce as much as possible. Then hand it to AI and improve it.

The first pass doesn't have to be good. It has to exist. You cannot improve something that isn't there yet.

*"Don't think. Just write. Just speak. Just go. The mess is the starting point — not the failure."*

**Method 3 — The Rapid Iteration Loop.**
Most learners: write → wait → get feedback days later → try again. The loop is too slow. By the time the feedback arrives, the moment is gone.

The right loop: produce → get immediate feedback → understand what to fix → do it again right now. Under 10 minutes. Same topic, better version, done.

This is what class looks like in practice: 2 minutes writing, paste into ChatGPT with a feedback prompt, read the feedback, write again for 2 minutes. Every loop moves you forward. Every loop takes minutes.

*"The people who improve fast aren't the ones who study more. They're the ones who iterate faster."*

---

**Part 4 — The Tools (4 min)**

The mindset and methods work. These tools make them work faster.

**Record yourself → transcribe → improve.**
Most people think about improving their speaking but never actually hear themselves speak. Record yourself — not the whole class, just yourself — upload it to a transcription tool, and read what you actually said. Then paste it into ChatGPT and ask: *"Show me how to improve this."*

You'll see things you never noticed: filler sounds, repeated words, awkward phrasing, grammar patterns you rely on. It's the most honest feedback you'll ever get because it's from yourself.

*"You can't fix what you can't hear. Record it. Read it. Fix it."*

**Two mini-prompts.**
Everything you don't understand → ChatGPT immediately. Two prompts that cover almost every situation:

- Phrase + `meaning` → ChatGPT explains what it actually means in context, not a dictionary definition
- Vietnamese sentence + `how to say` → natural English version, not a word-for-word translation

For writing feedback: *"Give me feedback on grammar and vocabulary — don't rewrite for me, just tell me what to fix."* The "don't rewrite" instruction is critical. It keeps the learning with you instead of in the AI's output.

**Build the reflex.**
The difference between people who use AI well and people who don't isn't knowledge — it's reflex. The habit of immediately reaching for the tool when something is unclear, instead of sitting and wondering or asking the person next to you.

*"The reflex that makes you good: anything unclear → ChatGPT immediately. Build that reflex."*

**Treat it like an employee.**
You are the manager. ChatGPT is your assistant. If your assistant is sitting there doing nothing — that's on you. You're not using your own resource. Not using it fully means your English doesn't grow as fast as it could. Simple as that.

*"ChatGPT is your employee. If they're sitting there doing nothing — that's on you."*

**Close (1 min)**

Come back to the opening: *"You've been studying English for years. Why aren't you fluent yet?"*

Now answer it properly: because you were given the wrong problems to solve, the wrong mindset to work from, and the wrong methods to use. None of that is your fault. But now you know.

The 1,000 hours are still required — there's no way around them. But the hours only count if the method is right.

*"There's no way around the hours. But there is a way to make the hours count more. That's what the last 25 minutes was."*
